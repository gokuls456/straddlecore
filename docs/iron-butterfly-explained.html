NIFTY 50 weekly options · intraday

StraddleCore Iron Butterfly

A hedged option-selling trade on NIFTY that opens at 10:00 AM and closes by 3:25 PM the same day. We built a backtester for it and a bot that sends every step to Telegram. The bot only sends alerts; it never places orders.

The idea

Get paid for a quiet day, with a cap on the bad ones

Option prices shrink through the day when the market doesn't move much. Selling the at-the-money call and put collects that premium. On its own that is risky, because a big move can cause a large loss. So we also buy two cheap options 300 points away on each side. Those "wings" put a ceiling on the loss and cut the margin from about ₹2.1 lakh to about ₹91,000 per lot.

The four legs, one lot each (65 qty)
SellATM Calle.g. 23300 CE
SellATM Pute.g. 23300 PE
BuyCall wing +300e.g. 23600 CE
BuyPut wing −300e.g. 23000 PE
The rules, in the order they happen

One trading day

  1. 10:00
    EnterSell the ATM call and put on the current weekly expiry, buy the ±300 wings.
  2. all day
    Stop-loss 20% per short legIf the sold call or put rises 20% above its entry price, close all positions.
  3. +15 min
    Re-enter onceWait 15 minutes, then sell a fresh ATM straddle at the new price, with a wider 40% stop-loss.
  4. every 10 s
    Portfolio guardIf total profit or loss for the day reaches ±10% of capital, close everything.
  5. 15:25
    ExitClose whatever is still open. Nothing is carried overnight.
Shape of the trade

Profit is highest when NIFTY stays near the strike

Example for one lot with a net credit of ₹240 (premium sold minus wings bought). This is the value at expiry, which shows the most the trade can make or lose. We exit the same afternoon, so a real day ends somewhere inside this shape, usually much closer to zero, and the stop-loss usually cuts losses well before the ₹3,900 floor.

Trust the 1-month row most. Free 5-minute NIFTY data only goes back about 60 days. Days before that are filled with simulated price paths that match each day's real open, high, low and close but move more smoothly than the real market. That suits a selling strategy, so longer periods look better than they really were: real candles give 59% win days, while mostly simulated ones give 87%.

Option prices are also estimated from NIFTY and India VIX, not taken from real historical option quotes. Charges are large: in the real-candle month they took ₹8,010 out of ₹16,449 gross profit.

Risks

What can go wrong

  • A strong trend day can hit the stop-loss, then hit the re-entry's stop-loss too. Those are the losing days in the table.
  • Fast markets fill stop-losses worse than the backtest assumes, especially around news, RBI policy and budget days.
  • About ₹91,000 of margin per lot is tied up to make the 1-month profit above, roughly 8% for the month on real candles.
  • One good month is a small sample. The real-candle result could easily be luck in either direction.
  • Selling options needs discipline: the strategy only works if every stop-loss and the 3:25 PM exit are taken without exception.
What we built

From idea to daily alerts

  • Backtest studio. A local web app that replays every day minute by minute, with the stop-loss, re-entry, guard and charges, and shows the equity curve and a day-by-day trade log.
  • Real exchange data. All prices now come from Dhan: real NIFTY 5-minute candles for backtests (up to 5 years) and live option prices for the alerts. The old free data's last candle of the day carried the official closing price instead of the last trade, which made 3:25 PM exits look wrong; Dhan's candles don't have that problem.
  • Telegram alerts every trading day. A scanner runs on GitHub Actions from 8:50 AM to 3:40 PM, Monday to Friday. Each run starts the next one, so it doesn't depend on GitHub's unreliable timer or on any computer staying on. Entries use live option prices from Dhan.
  • Alerts only. No broker account is connected for trading. Every alert is a suggestion to place the trade by hand.
Built for learning and testing. Backtest results are not a promise of future returns, and none of this is investment advice. Trade only with money you can afford to lose.
